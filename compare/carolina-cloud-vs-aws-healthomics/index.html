Carolina Cloud vs AWS HealthOmics

Should you use Carolina Cloud or AWS HealthOmics?

In our published benchmark, nf-core/rnaseq finished in 4.5 hours for $19.26 on Carolina Cloud, versus 5.5 hours and $45 on HealthOmics. If your organization requires pipelines to run inside AWS, under AWS compliance agreements and next to data in S3, HealthOmics keeps everything in one place.

AWS HealthOmics is a managed service for running Nextflow, WDL and CWL pipelines inside AWS. Carolina Cloud runs Nextflow with nf-ccloud, our own executor, which keeps workers warm and resizes them between tasks.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Carolina Cloud if…

  • You want nf-core pipelines to finish faster and cost less
  • You want to pull results out without egress fees
  • You want RStudio, JupyterLab and a genomics shell next to your pipeline outputs
  • You want help from engineers who run Nextflow themselves

Choose AWS HealthOmics if…

  • Your organization requires that genomic data stay inside your AWS account
  • You rely on AWS compliance agreements, IAM and VPC controls
  • You want Ready2Run pipelines or HealthOmics sequence and variant stores

Why it's faster

nf-ccloud keeps workers warm and resizes them between tasks, so a pipeline spends its time computing instead of waiting for machines to boot. Combined with lower per-core prices and no egress, total pipeline cost drops.

Frequently asked questions

How do I run Nextflow on Carolina Cloud?

Add the nf-ccloud plugin to your Nextflow run (-plugins nf-ccloud) with your Carolina Cloud API key. See our Nextflow page and blog for details.

Is Carolina Cloud cheaper than HealthOmics?

In our nf-core/rnaseq benchmark, $19.26 versus $45, and an hour faster. Your results will depend on the pipeline.

Can I keep my data in S3?

Yes. Carolina Cloud instances can read from S3 and our own storage is S3-compatible, though AWS will charge egress on data leaving S3.
