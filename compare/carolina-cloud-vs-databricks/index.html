Carolina Cloud vs Databricks

Should you use Carolina Cloud or Databricks?

If your team needs distributed Spark, governed data with Unity Catalog, or a shared lakehouse across a large organization, Databricks is worth it. If you mainly need notebooks on a powerful machine — pandas, Polars, DuckDB, R or single-node ML — Carolina Cloud is far cheaper and simpler.

Databricks is a managed lakehouse: Spark, Delta Lake, Unity Catalog, SQL warehouses and ML tooling on top of AWS, Azure or GCP. Carolina Cloud gives data scientists notebooks, JupyterLab and RStudio, on large dedicated-core machines, without a platform fee layered on top.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Carolina Cloud if…

  • Your data fits on one big machine (hundreds of GB of RAM goes a long way with Polars or DuckDB)
  • You want JupyterLab or RStudio in seconds, without cluster configuration
  • You want to stop paying a platform fee on top of compute
  • You work in bioinformatics or research and need Bioconductor and genomics tools preinstalled

Choose Databricks if…

  • You need distributed Spark over very large datasets
  • You need Unity Catalog governance, lineage and access control across a large organization
  • Your company has standardized on the lakehouse and SQL warehouses

Frequently asked questions

Is Carolina Cloud a Databricks alternative?

For notebook-based data science and single-node workloads, yes. For distributed Spark and governed lakehouse features, no. Databricks does things Carolina Cloud doesn't.

How does pricing differ?

Databricks charges DBUs, and on classic compute you also pay your cloud provider for VMs. Carolina Cloud charges only for the vCPUs, RAM and disk you use.
