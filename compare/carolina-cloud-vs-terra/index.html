Carolina Cloud vs Terra

Should you use Carolina Cloud or Terra for genomics analysis?

If your work depends on NIH consortium data in AnVIL or BioData Catalyst, or on WDL best-practice workflows like GATK and WARP, Terra is the natural home, and its platform layer is free. If you run Nextflow pipelines, want $0 egress, or want predictable prices without managing a Google Cloud billing account, Carolina Cloud fits better.

Terra is a research workspace platform from the Broad Institute, widely used in academia and NIH consortia such as AnVIL and BioData Catalyst. It runs WDL workflows with Cromwell and notebooks on Google Cloud, billed to your own Google billing account. Carolina Cloud runs Nextflow pipelines, notebooks and genomics tools on our own hardware.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Carolina Cloud if…

  • Your pipelines are written in Nextflow, including nf-core
  • You want $0 egress when you download results or share data
  • You want lower compute prices than Google Cloud list
  • You don't want to set up and manage a Google Cloud billing account

Choose Terra if…

  • You need NIH controlled-access or consortium data in AnVIL or BioData Catalyst
  • Your pipelines are WDL, such as GATK or WARP best-practice workflows
  • Your lab or consortium already collaborates in Terra workspaces
  • You want a free platform layer and are fine paying Google Cloud directly

Frequently asked questions

Is Terra free?

Terra charges no platform fee, but you pay Google Cloud for compute, storage and egress through your own billing account.

Can I run Nextflow on Terra?

Terra's native workflow language is WDL. Nextflow support has been announced as upcoming; check Terra's current documentation. On Carolina Cloud, Nextflow runs today via nf-ccloud.

Does Carolina Cloud charge egress?

No. Egress is $0.
